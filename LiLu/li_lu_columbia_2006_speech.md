## Executive Overview & Case Study Summary

In this guest lecture for Professor Bruce Greenwald's Value Investing seminar at Columbia Business School, Li Lu reflects on how hearing Warren Buffett speak in the same classroom 15 years earlier launched his investing career. He outlines the psychological and intellectual temperament required to belong to the fewer than 5% of market participants who practice true value investing, walks through three detailed case studies, and answers student questions on position sizing, mistakes of omission, tax-efficient compounding, and competitive moats.

## Section 3: The Temperament of the 5% — Evolutionary Mutation, Insatiable Curiosity & the Checklist ([00:15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=914s) – [00:19:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1151s))

**[00:15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=914s) Li Lu:** So my first point I want to leave with you is really to **understand who you are**, because you will be tested throughout your future career development. You're going to really have to face this and ask yourself whether you are a value investor or you are not a value investor.

**[00:15:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=936s) Li Lu:** Now, if for whatever reason your personality is built to be a value investor, that probably means that you somehow genetically mutated throughout the process of evolution:

1. **Comfort Being in the Minority:** First, you are very comfortable being in a minority, which is not really very natural to human beings. Throughout our human evolutionary process, most of us survived because we stuck with the group in the face of large mammals—that's the only way we could survive. So over tens of thousands of years of evolution, herd behavior is deeply ingrained into your genes. Occasionally, you will find a small percentage of people who survived despite the fact that they really have a different kind of gene—it's almost through a mutation process that that group of people survived. So the first thing is you have to really be very, very comfortable being very much by yourself. Therefore, you will naturally adopt the attitude that **you're right not because other people agree with you, but because your reasoning and your evidence are right**. It is common sense—but of course, as people say, common sense is the least common commodity. Most people don't think that way.
2. **Insatiable Curiosity as an Academic Researcher and Investigative Journalist:** Secondly, you will probably spend most of your time truly being an academic researcher instead of a so-called "professional investor." Most of the time you're going to spend as a value investor is really to be an academic, a researcher, an investigative journalist—to have an insatiable curiosity to try to figure out how just about everything works. In investing, really, the more you know, the better off you are as an investor. You just have to be naturally interested and curious about just about everything: any kind of business, politics, science, technology, humanities, history, poetry, literature—everything really affects your business. I don't want to scare you—you don't *have* to know everything, rapid mastery will just help you!

**[00:18:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1093s) Li Lu:** Occasionally, you will find a few insights out of those studies that give you tremendous opportunities that other people, for whatever reason, couldn't deal with—either psychologically (as somebody said), or because of the limitations of their thinking, or because of the institutional imperative of the organization they belong to.

**[00:18:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1120s) Li Lu:** Then you go through your **Four-Part Investment Checklist**:
1. **Valuation:** Is the business opportunity you're given cheap?
2. **Business Quality:** Is the business a good business?
3. **Management Integrity:** Is the management somebody that I can trust—either because they're good people or because the external checks are sufficient?
4. **Blind Spots ("What Did I Miss?"):** What else is missing, and why is this opportunity being presented to me?

Once you go through all of this checklist and you feel comfortable, then you just have to go over that last psychological barrier to basically do it.

### Rapid 5-Minute Valuation & Balance Sheet Breakdown

**[00:21:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1274s) Li Lu:** Now, when you look at this *Value Line* page for **Timberland** at $28 to $30, what is the first thing that jumps out at you? Somebody give me a quick read. Yes?

**[00:21:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1285s) Students:** *(Note the sharp price drop and low valuation relative to book value.)*

**[00:21:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1301s) Li Lu:** Now, if you're a value investor, you don't really care where it traded before. I'll tell you what I look for: I first look at **valuation**, and if the valuation doesn't fit, I don't even want to go beyond it. So what do we know about the valuation? Yes—it's trading near book value. And every time you see a stock near or below book value, you want to ask: **What's in the book? How much is the book?**

**[00:22:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1340s) Li Lu:** That's simple mental math: call the stock **$28**, and you've got roughly **11.5 million shares**, which is a market cap of **roughly $300 million** (low $300 millions—just approximate, you don't need a calculator). Now, what is in the book value?
- **Working Capital & Seasonal Retail Cash Conversion ([00:22:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1358s)):** The working capital is almost $300 million (roughly $275 million), and everything else largely cancels out. Of course, that balance sheet is at the end of Q3. In retail, what do you know about the end of Q3? This is where your quick encyclopedic knowledge really helps you: all retailers build up a huge amount of inventory in Q3 ahead of the holiday season, and they collect a lot of cash at the end of Q4. If you look back at the previous two years, you see they will probably collect about $100 million in cash at the end of the fourth quarter.
- **Liquid Assets vs. Fixed Assets ([00:23:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1408s)):** So out of roughly $300 million in book value, you've got about **$200 million in liquid assets** (roughly $100 million in cash plus $100 million in normal working capital) plus **$100 million in fixed assets**—and if you do a little bit more study, you'll see that $100 million is entirely buildings and real estate. So you're trading at roughly $300 million market cap, backed by $200 million in liquid assets and $100 million in real estate. You've got pretty decent protection on the downside!

**[00:23:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1432s) Li Lu:** Next, what do we know about the **earnings and cash flows**? The metric you want to pay the most attention to is **pre-tax, pre-interest earnings (unleveraged operating income / EBIT)**, and you want to compare that against the **unleveraged capital deployed** in the business to get a sense of what kind of business you have. How much are they making? Give me a quick sense.

**[00:24:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1458s) Li Lu:** If you're skilled, it shouldn't take you more than one second to find that out:
- **Operating Earnings (EBIT):** You've got roughly a **13% operating margin** on **$800 million to $850 million of sales**, so you get roughly **$100 million to $110 million** in pre-tax operating earnings.
- **Return on Deployed Capital ([00:24:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1475s)):** And how much capital is actually deployed in the business? Out of the $300 million book value ($200 million liquid assets + $100 million buildings), roughly $100 million of the liquid assets is excess cash. So you have roughly **$200 million of deployed capital** generating **$100 million to $110 million** of operating profit—a **return on deployed capital of roughly 50%**!

**[00:25:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1521s) Li Lu:** That's not a bad business! You give it a five-second look and say: "Hey, the business is trading right around book value; book value is very clean, consisting of tangible liquid working capital plus $100 million of real estate; the deployed capital is $200 million; and on that $200 million it returns roughly $100 million+ a year." If you think of yourself as a business owner and could buy the whole business at this price (~3x EBIT / ~5x net earnings), you'd probably want to own it—and Timberland is a widely recognized brand name.

### Investigating Why the Stock Collapsed & Why Wall Street Shunned It

**[00:26:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1563s) Li Lu:** So the next thing you check is: **Why did this whole thing fall apart at that point? What is missing?**
1. **Asian Financial Crisis Contagion ([00:26:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1595s)):** That was the height of the Asian Financial Crisis. All of their footwear and apparel peers—especially Nike and Reebok—saw their sales in Asia falling off a cliff. So the whole sector—all shoe retailers and international brands with any exposure to Asia—fell apart in sympathy.
2. **Zero Analyst Coverage ([00:27:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1624s)):** Next, you want to know what other people are thinking, so you check whether there are any Wall Street analyst reports. It turned out there were **no analyst reports—nobody covered this company**! For a company doing nearly $1 billion in sales with a major brand, why did nobody cover it? When you look back 10 to 15 years in *Value Line*, you see the business has been consistently profitable and growing, so its need for Wall Street financing was virtually zero. That's fabulous for you!
3. **Family Dual-Class Control ([00:28:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1696s)):** What's the ownership structure? It is family-owned *[the Swartz family]*. They own **40% of the equity**, and they control **98% of the voting power** (virtually 100% of the vote). Immediately, that turns off a whole bunch of institutional investors.
4. **Shareholder Lawsuits ([00:29:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1762s)):** Then you do a quick data search on how investors have reacted to them in the past, and you find there are a whole bunch of **shareholder lawsuits**.

**[00:29:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=1768s) Li Lu:** Think about that: you have a family owner with 40% of the stock and 98% of the vote; public shareholders have no vote; no Wall Street analysts cover them; and there are multiple shareholder lawsuits. ### Evolving on When to Sell: The Mathematics of the Government's Interest-Free Tax Loan

**[01:23:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=4996s) Student Question:** *(Asks how Li Lu decides when to sell a winner.)*

**[01:23:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=4998s) Li Lu:** My philosophy on selling has evolved. Early on, my rule was simple: *"If I wouldn't buy the stock at today's price, I sell it."*

**[01:23:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5012s) Li Lu:** Over time, I evolved because occasionally you find a rare business where the industry leader takes a disproportionate share of capital and its competitive advantage gets **stronger and stronger over time**. 1. **Reinvestment Risk:** You may never get another chance to buy that great business back.
2. **The 40%–50% Interest-Free Government Loan ([01:24:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5076s)):** Assuming you already have a huge unrealized gain, if you sell in a high-tax state like New York or California (federal + state/local taxes), you immediately hand over **40% to 50% of your gains in taxes**. Conversely, if you *don't* sell, **you are effectively leveraging your capital by 40% to 50% via an indefinite, non-callable, interest-free loan from the government**!
3. **High Return on Incremental Deployed Capital ([01:25:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5112s)):** If that business is able to redeploy its retained earnings at a **40% to 100% return on deployed capital** for the next 10 to 20 years, the compounding math of keeping that interest-free government tax loan inside a 50% ROE business becomes extraordinary.

**[01:25:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5134s) Li Lu:** Now, the caveat: Wall Street investment bankers project 5-year DCF models and "terminal values into infinity" every day, which is total bullshit—nobody can project an ordinary business even a few years out. When you own one of those rare businesses, **you don't need to sell**! ### Why Bloomberg Crushed Bridge and Reuters to Become a Winner-Take-All Monopoly

**[01:33:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5628s) Li Lu:** Let's talk about **Bloomberg** as a case study. When Michael Bloomberg started out (after leaving Salomon Brothers' bond desk), **Bridge** and **Reuters** were the long-established giants. 1. **Extreme Switching Costs via Opportunity Cost of Time ([01:35:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5709s)):** A student points out that the people who use Bloomberg terminals (traders and portfolio managers) have an extremely high opportunity cost of their time, and it takes a long time to learn all of Bloomberg's keyboard shortcuts and functionality. Li Lu calls this **the single best answer**: once you invest the time to master a complex tool that you rely on every single day to do your job, **you never want to learn another system again**.
2. **Bilateral Network Effects & University Seeding ([01:36:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5814s)):** Because everyone else on Wall Street uses Bloomberg, you must use it to communicate and collaborate with your partners, counterparties, and colleagues. By placing Bloomberg terminals cheaply or free inside every business school ([01:37:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5845s)), students learn Bloomberg before they even graduate—just like Microsoft Windows beat Apple when Apple once had dominant early share, because every employer used Windows ([01:38:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5900s)).
3. **Zero Marginal Cost, Custom Workflow Lock-In & No Menu ([01:38:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5935s)):** What is Bloomberg's marginal cost to add another terminal? Virtually zero! And how do they get traders hooked?
   - Bloomberg doesn't do equity research; instead, their reps visit traders almost every month and ask: *"What numbers or spreadsheets do you look at every day?"*
   - Traders (the 95%) are superstitious about their favorite indicators, so Bloomberg writes custom software analytics tailored specifically for that trader's daily routine ([01:39:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5965s)).
   - Bloomberg has tens of thousands of functions, and **they deliberately never give you a menu** ([01:39:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=5982s))! Instead, they get each of the 100,000+ financial professionals individually hooked on their own 4 or 5 custom functions in 100,000 different ways.
   - To a trader making or losing millions of dollars a day, paying **$30,000 a year** for a terminal—even when Bloomberg raises prices by **10% every year**—is nothing. You have no choice; you are hooked beyond repair ([01:40:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jF5au0-JiY&t=6010s)).
